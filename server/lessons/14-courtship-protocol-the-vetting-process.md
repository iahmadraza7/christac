Stage 4 - Date To the Ring

Courtship Protocol: The Vetting Process

SLIDE 1

Why the Vetting Process Exists

Throughout this entire course, you've been vetting him.

You've been watching how he leads, how he pursues, how he handles conflict, and whether his actions consistently match his words.

You've spent months discerning his character.

That has been your role.

Now that you're engaged, you aren't starting over.

You're adding the final layer.

Throughout this course, your responsibility has been discernment.

Now, the trusted men in your life step into their role.

Their job isn't to choose your husband.

Their job is to help protect one of the biggest decisions you'll ever make by verifying the tangible parts of the life he's built while also observing how he responds to loving accountability.

You can learn a great deal about someone's character through time and observation.

But there are also things that can—and should—be verified before entering a lifelong covenant.

This isn't about suspicion.

It isn't about assuming the worst.

And it isn't about putting a good man through unnecessary hoops.

It's about removing assumptions before marriage.

Wise women don't enter covenant in isolation.

They welcome loving accountability because they understand the significance of the commitment they're about to make.

A high-caliber man isn't threatened by that.

He welcomes it.

Because a man who has built a life with integrity has nothing to hide.

The Vetting Process doesn't replace your discernment.

It strengthens it.

This is the final layer of wisdom before your final yes.

SLIDE 2

Choosing Your Masculine Container

The Vetting Process isn't something you do alone.

It's something you invite trusted men into.

So who belongs in your masculine container?

These should be men you know and trust.

Men of integrity.

Men whose judgment you respect.

Men who have demonstrated wisdom through the way they've lived their own lives.

Most importantly, they should genuinely want what's best for you.

If your father is a healthy, trusted presence in your life, he would naturally be part of this process.

If not, that's okay.

Maybe it's an uncle.

A grandfather.

An older brother.

A mentor.

A pastor.

The title isn't what matters.

The character of the man does.

You're not looking for men who are intimidating.

You're not looking for men who enjoy confrontation.

And you're certainly not looking for men who are trying to prove something.

You're looking for men who are mature enough to be both discerning and gracious.

Men who understand the responsibility of protecting a woman they love.

Healthy masculinity doesn't seek control.

It provides protection.

That's the kind of men you want in your masculine container.

SLIDE 3

Preparing Your Masculine Container

Once you've chosen the men you'd like to be part of your masculine container, don't assume they automatically know what you're asking them to do.

Most won't.

Before they ever meet your fiancé, have a conversation with them.

Tell them why you've invited them into this process.

You might say something like:

"I've spent months discerning his character and the way he's led our relationship. I'm not asking you to make this decision for me. I'm asking you to help protect one of the biggest decisions I'll ever make."

Then explain their role.

Tell them:

"I'd like you to meet him, get to know him, verify the tangible parts of the life he's shared with me, and give me your honest perspective before we move forward with marriage."

Next, prepare a verification list.

Think through the significant parts of his life that he's represented throughout your relationship.

For example:

Has he represented that he owns a home?

Has he shared that he owns a business?

Has he talked about degrees, licenses, or professional credentials?

Has he shared that he's divorced?

Has he discussed retirement planning or other long-term financial preparations?

Are there any other significant parts of his life that are important to verify before marriage?

Share that list with your masculine container before the meeting.

Then give them permission to add to it.

These men bring a perspective that complements your own.

They may think of questions you never would have considered.

That's one of the reasons you've invited them into this process.

Finally, prepare them for the tone of the meeting.

Tell them:

"I'm not asking you to interrogate him or try to catch him in something. I want this to feel respectful, and welcoming. I simply want you to verify what can be verified, get to know him, and give me your honest feedback afterward."

When everyone understands both the purpose and the posture of the meeting, it creates an environment where honesty, wisdom, and accountability can naturally flourish.

SLIDE 4

The Vetting Meeting Part 1

Now it's time for the meeting.

This should be a private conversation between your fiancé and the trusted men in your masculine container.

The tone should be respectful.

It should be welcoming.

But it should also be intentional.

This isn't a casual dinner or a social visit.

The men in your masculine container have been entrusted with helping protect one of the biggest decisions you'll ever make, and they should approach that responsibility accordingly.

Before the meeting begins, there are two important things you need to know.

First, do not tell the men in your masculine container that they're meeting with him because you asked them to.

This meeting isn't about you testing him. It's about the men who love you stepping into their responsibility to protect you as you consider the man who may become your husband.

Second, this meeting should be led by men.

Don't substitute your best friend, your sister, or another woman. There's a different level of respect, accountability, and understanding that naturally exists in a man-to-man conversation, and that's what makes this process so effective.

SLIDE 5

The Vetting Meeting Part 2

One of the men may choose to begin the meeting by saying something like:

"We're grateful you took the time to meet with us. We've heard wonderful things about you, and we're looking forward to getting to know you better.

We love her deeply, and because we love her, we take seriously the responsibility of protecting the women entrusted to our care.

Before we bless this marriage, there are some things we'd like to walk through together. We'll ask questions, we'll ask you to provide documentation for certain parts of your life, and we'll spend some time getting to know the man who's asking to lead her for the rest of her life.

At that point, the meeting begins.

The masculine container should have the verification list in front of them.

They should intentionally work through each item together.

If she has asked them to verify his divorce, they should ask.

If she has asked them to verify his business ownership, they should ask.

If she has asked them to verify his education, professional licenses, home ownership, retirement planning, or any other significant part of the life he's represented, they should ask.

This isn't optional.

This is the responsibility she has entrusted them with.

As each item is discussed, they should ask him to provide the appropriate documentation following the meeting so those representations can be verified.

As the meeting continues, they're paying attention to far more than the information itself.

They're watching the man.

How does he respond when accountability is introduced?

Does he welcome the process?

Does he answer honestly?

Is he respectful?

Is he secure enough to understand why this meeting exists?

Healthy, confident men generally understand this.

In many cases, if the roles were reversed and this were their daughter, sister, or another woman they deeply loved, they would likely expect the very same thing.

This meeting communicates something important.

It tells him that the woman he's asking to marry is deeply loved.

She's protected.

She has men in her life who care enough to ensure she's entering marriage with wisdom rather than assumptions.

A man who genuinely desires to marry her should respect that.

Because this isn't about proving his worth.

It's about demonstrating that the life he's presented is the life he's actually built.

When the meeting concludes, the masculine container should have requested what they need to complete the verification process, reflect on what they've observed, and prepare to offer her honest counsel.

SLIDE 6

When Something Doesn't Match

Sometimes the verification process uncovers information that doesn't match what you've been told.

If that information reveals deception, significant omissions, or a pattern of dishonesty, don't ignore it.

This process exists to protect you from entering a lifelong union with a man whose character doesn't support the marriage he's asking you to build.

Not every discrepancy is a deal breaker.

Sometimes there's a reasonable explanation.

That's why you pause.

Give your masculine container time to complete the verification process, discuss what they've found, and share their perspective with you.

Then listen carefully.

The goal isn't to look for reasons to end the relationship.

The goal is to make sure you're not overlooking reasons you should.

SLIDE 7 - The Blessing

Once the verification process is complete, it's time for your masculine container to share their perspective.

Listen carefully.

These are men you chose because you trust their wisdom, judgment, and character.

If they confidently bless the marriage, receive that blessing with gratitude.

If they have concerns, don't dismiss them.

Lean in.

Ask questions.

Seek understanding.

That's why you invited them into this process.

Ultimately, the decision is yours.

But wisdom welcomes trusted counsel before making a lifelong covenant.

SLIDE 8

Don't Skip the Final Step

By now, you may be thinking,

"This feels like a lot. Can't I just enjoy being engaged?"

I understand.

But don't skip this final step.

Everything you've learned throughout this course has been designed to help you choose a husband wisely—not simply become a wife quickly.

An engagement doesn't mean the pursuit is over.

It means you're one step closer to a lifelong covenant.

This final step reminds him that marriage isn't something he's receives simply because he proposed.

It's something he continues to earn through character, integrity, and consistency.

When a man continues to earn you, he values you more deeply.

And what a man values, he's far more likely to protect, cherish, and steward well.

Don't rush the finish line.

Finish well.
